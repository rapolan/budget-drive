## 0. Built Features

Everything in this section is shipped and running today, distinct from the patent-pending research/roadmap sections below.

### Person and Enrollments
A student is a **person** — identity, address, date of birth, guardians, permit — who can hold one or more **enrollments**, each exactly one program: **driver education** (30 hours, classroom/online) or **driver training** (6 hours, behind-the-wheel). A person may take either, both, or one here and one elsewhere, sometimes years apart; a returning student can start a second `driver_training` enrollment once their first one has completed (e.g. car training now, motorcycle training years later). This session builds `driver_training` fully — hours, progress, completion, lessons, fee flags. `driver_education` exists as a program type with manually entered completion (date + hours) only — no lesson tracking or scheduling for it yet. See `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §11 for the full schema.

Certificates are issued per program, on different DMV form types — a person who completes both programs gets two certificates. Certificates attach to the **enrollment**, not the person — see "Certificate Issuance Tracking" below and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §13.

### Jurisdiction-Aware Student Progress
Progress is derived live at read time from a student's **active driver_training enrollment**, never stored as a running total (no cached hours-completed column exists anywhere — carrying one forward when program state moved off `students` was deliberately avoided, for the same reason the old `students.total_hours_completed` was dead: a stored counter drifts the moment a lesson is edited, deleted, or re-marked). A student's age is computed from `date_of_birth` (stays on the person, not the enrollment): minors progress on an **hours track** against the enrollment's `hours_required`, defaulted from a tenant-configurable `default_hours_required`; adults (18+) have no mandated hours and progress on a **lessons track** (booked vs. completed lesson count). `computeStudentProgress` in `backend/src/services/studentProgressService.ts` is the single source of truth — every UI surface (student list, cards, detail view) consumes its output rather than recomputing; this didn't change when its input moved from the student row to an enrollment. An admin can pin a student's enrollment to either track via `track_override`, independent of age. A student whose only `driver_training` enrollment has completed, with no new one started, shows "No active enrollment" rather than a blank or zero progress bar — a real, common state, not an error.

**Display rule — one language in the list, hours preserved on the record.** The Students list (table and card views, both rendered through the single shared `StudentProgressBar` component) always speaks in **lesson counts** — `"X / Y lessons"` — for every student regardless of track, since mixing "X of Y hrs" and "X of Y lessons" in the same list read inconsistently. For a minor, `Y` is `lessonsRequired`, derived by `computeStudentProgress` as `ceil(hoursRequired * 60 / standardLessonLengthMinutes)` (a new tenant setting, default 120 minutes — see ARCHITECTURE.md §6). The underlying **hours figure is never dropped**: it remains on the `StudentProgress` payload (`hoursCompleted`/`hoursRequired`/`hoursScheduled`), stays visible on the student detail record alongside the lesson count (it's the legally binding number for California minors, not the lesson count), and is still what the turning-18 alert compares against. Because lesson durations vary, a minor can satisfy their required lesson *count* while still short of their required *hours* (three 90-minute lessons is 4.5 of a 6-hour requirement) — the detail record flags this mismatch explicitly so a program is never marked complete on lesson count alone.

### Turning-18 Alerts
The dashboard surfaces students who are 18+ but still mid-program on the hours track with unbooked required hours remaining, so an admin can consciously decide whether to keep them on the hours track, switch them to the lessons track, or mark the program complete — a decision the system never makes automatically.

### No-Show Follow-Up Alerts
Marking a lesson `no_show` creates a dismissable notification (using the platform's existing, previously-unused `notifications` table) surfaced on the dashboard. The alert clears automatically once that student's next lesson is booked, or can be dismissed manually.

### Program Completion with Verification
Completion is an explicit admin action on an enrollment (`POST /enrollments/:id/complete`) with an optional reason, not an automatic hours-threshold inference — an admin verifies and records why a program ended. Reversing it (`POST /enrollments/:id/reopen`) is a **guarded** write, not a plain undo: it requires a non-empty reason, is restricted to owner/admin roles, and records who reopened it, when, and why — the completion record itself (including its BSV-forward-compatibility hash, see §4) is never erased, only marked reopened. If a certificate was already issued for that enrollment, the reopen response says so explicitly (now scoped precisely to that enrollment's own certificate, not just any certificate the person happens to have — see Certificate Issuance Tracking below) so the certificate can be handled separately; the endpoint does not touch it.

### Enrollment Withdrawal
A minor who leaves a program before completing it is entitled under 13 CCR §340.27 to a transcript of training received. `withdrawn` is a distinct enrollment status from `inactive`/`suspended` — those don't mean "the student left," this specifically does. `POST /enrollments/:id/withdraw` is a **guarded** write with the same shape as reopen: requires a non-empty reason, restricted to owner/admin, records who/when/why, and is only callable on an `active` enrollment — completion and withdrawal are mutually exclusive outcomes for one enrollment, never both.

### Certificate Issuance Tracking
California driving schools issue DMV certificates of completion (DL 400D for behind-the-wheel driver training; DL 400B/DL 400C for classroom/online driver education, resolved from the enrollment's delivery mode — see "Driver Education Tracking Module" below) to minors who complete their program. 13 CCR §340.27 requires each certificate recorded in numerical sequence with student name and issue date, a void/lost/stolen accounting (the DL 803 log), and — for a minor who withdraws before completing — a transcript of training received on request.

**The real workflow, not an idealized one.** Instructors hold physical certificates and hand one to a student at their final lesson, writing the serial on the student's paper record sheet. Sheets come back to the admin, who works through the stack entering each serial against the enrollment it belongs to. The app is a **reconciliation system of record** — it does not issue certificates live at program completion. The admin's core task is working down a stack of returned sheets, so the **awaiting-certificate worklist** is the center of this feature, not a side panel.

Driver Education certificates live on this same page, behind a Behind-the-Wheel / Driver Education tab — but the workflow underneath the DE tab is genuinely different, not a re-skin. DE is **immediate issuance**: a student surfaces the moment their cohort finishes (classroom) or their online module is done, and the school issues right then, so the DE tab's worklist is framed as "Ready for issuance," not "awaiting sheets." The certificate row, the issued/void log, the digital view, and void handling are all the exact same one dataset as BTW, just filtered — nothing here forks into a second system.

- **The BTW worklist** lists completed `driver_training` enrollments with no certificate recorded yet, filtered to students who were **minors as of their enrollment's own completion date** — not today's date, so a student who has since turned 18 still surfaces if they were a minor when the program actually ended. Each row expands into an inline form: serial number, issue date (pre-filled to the completion date, editable), and a suggested issuing instructor (pre-filled from that enrollment's most recent completed lesson, editable — the sheet is always the source of truth). Recording drops the row off the worklist immediately.
- **The DE worklist** shows two kinds of row, same minors-only rule. A student who already completed with no certificate yet (online's manual completion, or a classroom student completed without issuing right away) gets the identical plain "Record certificate" form the BTW worklist uses. A classroom student who just hit 4/4 curriculum days but hasn't been marked complete yet gets a combined **"Complete & issue certificate"** action instead — one click completes the enrollment and records the certificate together, atomically, since attendance completion is something the system can detect on its own and there's no reason to make that two separate trips. Online DE has no such detectable moment, so it has no combined action — it's completed manually (the same "Mark complete" action BTW enrollments use, now available to DE too) and then flows through the plain worklist row like any other completed-but-uncertified enrollment.
- **Recording is gated by whether a certificate was issued, never by age.** The worklist's minors-only filter decides what *surfaces* there — it is not a permission check. Any completed enrollment, adult or minor, worklist-listed or not, can have a certificate recorded directly from its own record on the student page, via the identical form.
- **Void** — a page-header action for a spoiled, lost, or stolen certificate that never reached a student: a serial and a required reason, no enrollment, no student. This is the §340.27/DL 803 accounting, tracked in the same table and surfaced in its own count tile alongside Issued, and unaffected by which program tab is active — a void isn't attached to a program.
- **The issued/void log is one list, filterable by program** — a dropdown narrows it to Behind-the-Wheel or Driver Education certificates, alongside the existing instructor filter, but it's a view filter over the same underlying log, never a separate log per program.
- **On the student record**, a recorded certificate shows next to its enrollment's completion status as a gold, `Award`-icon badge (serial, issue date, status). A completed minor enrollment with no certificate yet shows a muted "Awaiting certificate" state — the same eligibility rule as the worklist, so the two views can never disagree; a completed adult enrollment shows neither badge by default, since it isn't awaiting anything unless a certificate is actually recorded.
- **Withdrawal training-received transcript** — generated on demand (not queued) from an enrollment's own record: a plain-text document listing the enrollment, every lesson (date, duration, instructor, status), total hours completed, and — when the enrollment is withdrawn — the withdrawal date and reason. No age check, and not restricted to withdrawn enrollments specifically: available for any `driver_training` enrollment that isn't completed, since a training-received record is always a safe thing to produce on request.
- **Two different people are recorded on a certificate, deliberately never collapsed into one column**: the instructor who physically issued it, and the admin who later entered its serial into the app from the returned sheet — often different people, sometimes different days.
- **Digital certificate view** — a "View" action on an issued log row opens a clean digital reference copy of the certificate's content (school identity + DMV license number, student, instructor + license number, form type, serial, dates), reachable and printable (a Print button opens a bare, chrome-free page in a new tab). This is explicitly **not** the official form — the physical DL 400D/DL 400B/DL 400C the school prints and hands to the student remains the authoritative document; this is an internal copy of the record, and it renders correctly for any of the three form types without any extra code, since the document view was already form-type-agnostic.

### Driver Education Tracking Module
An administrative tracking tool (not a course-delivery platform) for the classroom driver education the school teaches itself — a 30-hour, 4-day course, each day covering distinct material. Off by default per tenant, on the Classroom nav page once enabled. A student's completion is never tied to finishing one class start-to-finish: it's the union of attended days across ANY class the school runs, which is what lets a student who misses a day make it up at a different class's session covering that same day, without losing credit for days already attended. Online driver education (self-paced, completion entered manually) has its own home on the same Classroom page now — see "Finishing the Lifecycle" below.

The Classroom page is a cohort list (left) and the selected class's roster (right) — students down the side, its 4 class dates across the top, a checkbox per (student, day). An "Add make-up" picker on each day lets the admin add a student attending as a guest from a different home class; a "Missing N days" badge tells the admin at a glance who still needs a make-up, computed across the student's attendance everywhere, not just this one class. Adding a `driver_education` enrollment from a student's record now asks Classroom or Online first — Classroom joins an existing upcoming class (never creates one; that's a Classroom-page-only action), Online keeps the original manually-entered-hours flow.

**Choosing the program at intake.** Creating a student now starts with an "Enrolling in" choice - Behind-the-Wheel or Driver Education - instead of always defaulting to Behind-the-Wheel and requiring a separate enrollment step afterward. Behind-the-Wheel is the same form as always, unchanged. Driver Education reveals Classroom or Online; Classroom additionally shows an inline list of upcoming classes with their dates, teacher, and remaining capacity (color-coded so a nearly-full or full class is obvious at a glance) - picking one enrolls the new student into that class the moment they're created, in one "Create & enroll" action. The class is optional - leaving it unpicked creates the student and their Driver Education enrollment, to be assigned to a class later. This only sets the student's FIRST enrollment; the enrollment tab's "add another enrollment later" is still there for a student who goes on to do both programs.

**The form only asks for what the chosen program needs.** A Driver Education student hasn't gotten their learner's permit yet - they earn one by completing DE, and the permit is then what lets them start Behind-the-Wheel - so the permit fields (number, issue date, expiration) and the training-hours field simply don't appear while creating a DE student; they're not disabled, they're not shown at all. Switching the toggle to Behind-the-Wheel brings them back. A DE student's permit fields stay blank (not a placeholder value) until they later add a BTW enrollment - see "Enrolling a Driver Education graduate in Behind-the-Wheel" below, which is exactly that moment - and are always editable any time from the student's own record after that. Home address is still there for a DE student (worth recording if known) but no longer needs to be filled in to create the student; for Behind-the-Wheel, where the address is actually used for pickup scheduling, nothing changes.

### The Students List Speaks Both Programs
The Students list used to assume every student was a Behind-the-Wheel student - a Driver Education student with no BTW enrollment showed "No Active Enrollment," reading as if they were overdue to book a lesson they'd never signed up for. The list is now organized as three tabs above it - Behind-the-Wheel, Driver Education, and All - and switching tabs changes the whole page to that program's vocabulary at once, not just which rows show up. Behind-the-Wheel looks exactly as it always has: its own stat cards, its own status filters, lesson-and-hours progress bars. Driver Education gets its own four cards - In a Class, Unassigned, Completed, and Awaiting Cert (a completed student whose certificate hasn't been recorded yet) - its own matching filters, and a Class column showing which cohort a classroom student belongs to instead of a progress bar that wouldn't mean anything for DE's day-by-day structure. All is the plain, simple list of everyone, with no cards of its own, showing each student's furthest-along status - Behind-the-Wheel if they've reached it (Driver Education being behind them in the usual sequence), otherwise Driver Education. A small Program column ("DE", "BTW", or "DE·BTW") marks who's in which on every tab, and a student in both programs still shows up as one row, never duplicated. All is also where the page opens by default, so nobody is ever hidden behind a program tab they haven't chosen yet; switching to a specific tab is a deliberate choice to narrow in, not something the page decides for you. Each tab remembers its own filter selection, so leaving Behind-the-Wheel on "Needs Attention" and coming back to it later - after checking in on Driver Education - doesn't reset it.

### Enrolling a Driver Education Graduate in Behind-the-Wheel
A student's record now has a dedicated "Enroll in BTW" action once they have no active Behind-the-Wheel enrollment, replacing the old plain text link. It's ready to go the moment the student has finished Driver Education - here, or (with a checkbox to record where and when) elsewhere - and otherwise shows a note that DE completion is expected first, without ever blocking an admin who knows the student is ready anyway. Enrolling captures the learner's permit right there in the same step - the natural moment to record it, since finishing DE is what makes the permit possible in the first place - though it's never required and stays editable on the student's record afterward regardless. Everything submitted together - the new enrollment, the permit, and (if used) the note about DE completed elsewhere - is saved as one all-or-nothing action, so a mid-save hiccup can never leave a half-finished enrollment behind. This only ever runs in the Driver-Education-to-Behind-the-Wheel direction; there's no equivalent action offered in reverse.

**Adding a guardian starts light.** The guardian section opens with just a name to fill in. The moment an admin starts typing a guardian's name, the section expands to also ask for that guardian's phone and email - so a real point of contact gets captured, not just a name, but nothing clutters the form before the admin has actually started adding someone. That captured phone or email is the same one already used everywhere else a guardian's contact shows up - including as the fallback contact shown for a minor with no phone or email of their own.

**Adding a student straight from the roster.** Joining a class no longer requires leaving the Classroom page to go through a student's own record. An "Add student" button on the roster opens a panel with two tabs. "Existing student" searches the tenant's students and shows, per result, their age (minor/adult) and where they stand with this class: no driver education enrollment yet, addable now, already on this roster (disabled), or already enrolled in a *different* class — clearly named, so the admin isn't left guessing which one, and blocked rather than silently creating a conflicting membership. "New student" doesn't duplicate the create-student form — it opens the same intake form above, pre-set to Driver Education / Classroom / this class, so the same one "Create & enroll in [class name]" action handles it. The panel respects the class's capacity (remaining spots shown, adding blocked once full) and the roster updates immediately once a student is added, ready to mark attendance across all 4 days.

A student who completes the school's own (internal) driver education earns an automatic, editable discount ($5 default) on their behind-the-wheel lessons — applied server-side at booking time, recorded per-lesson for auditability, never a silent price change. A driving school license number obtained elsewhere (`external_de_completed`, on the driver_training enrollment) does not qualify — only DE actually completed in this system does.

The DE-before-BTW sequencing many schools prefer (finish classroom driver education, then start behind-the-wheel) is not enforced by the system - an admin can enroll a student in either program, or both, in any order. That prerequisite is treated as something the admin already knows and handles on the intake call, not a rule the software gets in the way of.

See `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §14 for the full schema, completion-model, and discount rationale, and `docs/compliance-records-build-plan.md` for the phase's build history — all 7 commits are built and live-verified end-to-end.

### Finishing the Lifecycle

The DE module's happy path - enroll, attend, complete, certify - worked, but the edges around it didn't: a bug quietly swallowed adult and online completions, and three everyday admin moments (a student cancelling out of a class, a class wrapping up, online students finishing at all) had nowhere to go.

**Online completions were invisible on the certificate worklist - fixed.** Marking an online DE student complete always worked; the record genuinely saved. But the "ready to issue" list on the Certificates page quietly filtered out anyone who wasn't a minor, a rule that belongs to Behind-the-Wheel's own §340.27 consent requirement and was copied over by mistake when the DE worklist was first built. An adult who finished DE - the common case for online - would complete successfully and then simply never show up anywhere asking for their certificate. That filter is gone now: DE's worklist shows every finished student, the same way recording a DE certificate has never actually cared about age.

**Online DE now has a home to finish in.** A classroom student finishes by attendance - 4 checked boxes and they're done. An online student had no equivalent: their hours-logged number was a read-only line buried on their own record, with no list of who's in progress and no way to update that number after the day they enrolled. The Classroom page now has an Online tab alongside its class list - every online DE student still working toward their hours, with an inline box to log hours as they go and a "Mark complete" button once they're done. That button is the exact same "mark complete" the classroom side already uses - completing here lands a student on the certificate worklist exactly the way finishing a class does, one path, not two.

**A student can now be removed from a class.** Someone who cancels before a class starts had no way off the roster. "Remove" ends their membership in that specific class - the days they've actually attended stay recorded and still count if they join a different class later; only "this is their home class" is undone, never their history.

**Closing out a class is now its own step.** When a class's four days are behind it, "Close class" shows a plain summary - who finished all four days, who's still short and by how many - before marking the class done. It never marks a student complete on its own; completion still only ever comes from attendance. Closing just makes the wrap-up visible instead of something an admin had to notice on their own.

**Creating a student straight into a nearly-full class is now recoverable.** If two admins are both enrolling a student into the same class at once, the second one can lose the last spot - the student still gets created, just without a class. Rather than leaving that admin to go hunt the student down on the Classroom page, the same screen now offers "Try a different class" right there, so the enrollment gets finished in the same breath instead of becoming a loose end.

**A finished Driver Education student can now be marked complete from the Students list itself, not only from the Classroom page.** The list's "Mark complete" action only ever understood Behind-the-Wheel progress - a Driver Education student who'd finished all 4 class days, or was ready to finish online, had no complete button anywhere on the Students page even though they were done. The Driver Education tab now has its own matching action, using the exact same "finished all 4 days" or "ready to finish online" facts already shown elsewhere on the page - never a different opinion about who's done - and the same guarded "are you sure" step Behind-the-Wheel's button already uses. It marks the enrollment complete the identical way every other completion in the app does, so it lands on the certificate worklist right afterward just like finishing from the Classroom page would. Behind-the-Wheel's button still only shows on the Behind-the-Wheel tab, and this new one only on the Driver Education tab - each tab's complete action matches its own program.

**"Mark contacted" is fixed - it was silently failing on every use.** The button existed, looked like it worked, and quietly did nothing: the database was never actually set up to store when a student was last contacted, so every attempt failed behind the scenes with no visible error. It's a one-line database fix - nothing about how the button itself worked needed to change - and marking a student contacted now genuinely saves and clears their follow-up flag as intended.

**A finished class now has a permanent, browsable record.** Closing a class used to just change its status with nothing to look back at afterward. The Classroom page now keeps a "Completed classes" section below the active list - every closed class, most recent first - where opening one shows exactly who was on the roster and how they finished, precisely as it stood the day the class closed. It's read-only by design: nothing on a closed class can be edited or undone from here, the same look-back-only treatment the Certificates page already gives issued certificates.

**Online Driver Education students now have a "done" list to match their "in progress" one.** The Online tab always showed who was still working toward finishing; it now also shows who already has, right below - name, hours logged, and the date they finished, no actions needed since a certificate is handled entirely through the certificate worklist already covered above.

See `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §14 for the exact root causes, the schema (none of this needed new columns), and how each fix was live-verified.

### Archive

Finished students eventually recede from the daily working views - the active Students list, its filter counts - not because their records are deleted, but because they're filed away. Everything about them stays exactly as it was: fully retained, fully searchable, fully retrievable, for the compliance period the law requires. Archiving is a soft, reversible flag on the student, not a move to a different table and not a deletion.

**A student is ready to archive once their record is genuinely at rest** - completed, and then quiet for a while, in a way that depends on which program they finished. A Behind-the-Wheel student whose learner's permit has expired is the clearest signal: the permit was the whole reason they could keep training, so once it's gone and nothing new has happened since, the record is done. An adult with no permit on file gets a fallback instead - a stretch of true inactivity, long enough that it's clearly over rather than just a pause between lessons (tunable per school, since what counts as "clearly over" varies). A Driver Education student is simpler: once their completion falls in a year that's now behind us, they're ready - DE completion doesn't come back around the way a BTW student sometimes drifts back in for one more lesson. An incomplete record is never touched by any of this, no matter how long it's been sitting - a lapsed permit on someone who never finished just means "may renew and return," and stays in the working list where an admin will actually see it.

**Nothing gets archived without someone deciding to.** This app has learned the hard way that automatic behavior which happens quietly, with no one watching, is exactly the kind of thing that goes wrong unnoticed. So eligibility is just a worklist - a fresh, honest answer to "who could be archived right now, and why" every time the page loads - and archiving itself is always a click: one student at a time, or "archive all eligible" as one deliberate batch action. An admin who knows better can jump the queue too, sealing a record early the moment they know a student is truly done, right from that student's own page.

**A known returner can be held out of all of this by name.** Marking a student "held" - with a short reason, so it means something later - keeps them off the eligibility list indefinitely; there's no timer quietly working against that decision. Held students aren't hidden, though - they live on their own reviewable list, so a hold from eighteen months ago doesn't just evaporate into forgotten limbo. Clearing a hold doesn't archive anyone by itself; it just lets that student be considered again, the normal way.

**Archived never means lost.** Search for a student by name and an archived one still turns up, clearly tagged, sitting right alongside everyone else in the results - never silently filtered out. Click into them and their whole record opens exactly as it always did: every lesson, every payment, every certificate, all of it. A former student who calls asking for a copy of their certificate, or a transcript of their training, gets one - archived status never stands in the way of either.

**The Archive page** is where sealed records live, organized the way a filing cabinet is - by year, then by month within it, most recent first, with older years tucked away collapsed until someone wants to look. Each record there can be restored to active with one guarded confirmation, since undoing a seal is worth pausing over.

**Sealing writes a fingerprint of the record's provable facts** - not the record itself, and never anything personal. No name, no email, no address - just the IDs, dates, hours, and certificate numbers that together prove what was actually done, plus which licensed school did the sealing. That fingerprint doesn't go anywhere yet - it's recorded and shown as "not yet anchored" today - but it's built the same way this system already prepares its certificates and completions for the day blockchain verification comes online, so a future update can make that proof checkable without touching a single record again. If a restored student later finishes something new and gets re-archived, the fingerprint is simply recomputed - an honest record of the most recent true state, not a pile of stale ones.

See `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §16 for the full schema, eligibility rules, and hash composition, and `docs/compliance-records-build-plan.md` for the phase's build history.

### Recording a Payment

This is manual bookkeeping, not a payment processor - an admin is recording what already happened (cash handed over, a Venmo transfer received), not charging a card. A real processor integration (Stripe, Square) is a deliberately separate future phase.

**The whole action was broken before this - it just failed silently under the hood.** Recording a payment always 500'd, on every attempt, regardless of anything the admin picked - the database was never actually set up to track who recorded or last touched a payment, even though the code already assumed it could. That's fixed with no visible change to how it's used; recording a payment now genuinely works.

**The amount shows up already filled in, front and center.** The moment a student is picked, the amount defaults to exactly what they still owe - a big, bold number the admin can tap into and change if this payment isn't for the full balance, with a small note underneath explaining where the number came from. Picking a different student updates it again, unless the admin has already started typing their own number - once they have, their edit is never overwritten out from under them.

**Six payment methods, shown as one-tap chips instead of a dropdown**: Card, Cash, Venmo, Zelle, PayPal, and Check, all visible at once. Two more - BSV and MNEE - sit at the end of the row, greyed out with a small lock icon until blockchain payments are turned on for the school in Settings; flip that on and they become normal, selectable chips like everything else. Card records as a card payment - not a real Stripe charge, since nothing here processes real cards yet.

**An optional reference number** - a receipt number, a check number, the last few digits of a card - can be jotted down alongside the payment, for whenever someone needs to trace it back to a physical slip or statement later. It shows up right in that student's payment history alongside everything else about the payment.

**A running balance updates live as the amount changes**, sitting just above the button in its own quiet highlighted strip - "New balance after this payment: $X.XX" - so the admin can see the effect of what they're about to record before they confirm it. If the amount happens to be more than what's owed, the balance doesn't just stop at zero and hide that fact - it shows the extra as a credit, since that's real money the student now has sitting toward whatever they owe next.

**One big button, and nothing to double-check afterward.** A single full-width "Record Payment" button is the only primary action, with a small line underneath reminding the admin that everything's already filled in and ready - they can just confirm, or adjust anything first. It disables itself the instant it's tapped, so an impatient extra tap can't record the same payment twice, and the modal closing is the confirmation that it worked.

See `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §17 for the schema fixes, the exact method-to-database mapping, and how each piece was live-verified.

### Pricing Driver Education

Driver Education had no price tag at all until now - a DE student could be enrolled, attend, and complete the whole program with no cost ever recorded, and no way to record a payment against them even if there had been one. Behind-the-Wheel has always billed by the lesson; Driver Education bills differently - one flat course fee, charged once at enrollment, not per class day.

**A course fee shows up the moment Driver Education is chosen**, defaulting to the school's own price for whichever delivery mode is picked - classroom and online each have their own configurable default, editable in Settings alongside the school's other pricing defaults. The fee is right there to review or change before the student is even created, the same "already filled in, still yours to adjust" feel Behind-the-Wheel's own lesson cost has always had.

**That fee becomes a real, payable balance immediately.** A newly-enrolled classroom student with the default $150 fee and nothing paid shows a $150 balance the moment they're created - on their own record, and now on the Payments page too. Recording a payment against them pre-fills that same $150, same as it would for a Behind-the-Wheel student's lesson balance, and paying it off brings their balance to zero exactly the way it should.

**A person who owes on both programs at once now shows one combined number**, not just whichever program happened to be tracked before. Behind-the-Wheel debt and Driver Education debt add together into a single balance and a single status, so nothing about what a student actually owes goes missing just because they're partway through both programs.

**The existing Driver-Education-graduate discount on Behind-the-Wheel lessons is untouched** - it still only ever knocks a few dollars off a lesson's cost for someone who finished this school's own Driver Education, and it still never touches the Driver Education fee itself. Nothing about how that discount works changed here.

### Three Bugs From One Real Walkthrough

Actually creating a Driver Education student - a minor, with a guardian, the way it happens in real life - surfaced three problems in a row that a narrower test never would have caught.

**A Driver Education choice was quietly getting lost for minors.** Creating a minor always goes through the same step as adding their guardian, in one action - and on that specific path, the Driver Education choice never actually made it through. The student got created fine, the guardian got linked fine, but the enrollment came out as Behind-the-Wheel regardless of what was actually picked. Adults, who don't need this guardian step, were never affected - which is exactly why this went unnoticed until someone walked through the minor case for real.

**Adding that same student to a class then failed** - and it turned out to be the very same problem showing up a second time, not a new one. Once she was actually enrolled in the right program (Driver Education, not Behind-the-Wheel), adding her to a class worked immediately - the class-adding logic itself had nothing wrong with it, it was correctly rejecting a Behind-the-Wheel student the whole time.

**The confirmation after creating a student never actually looked at which program was chosen.** Every new student - Behind-the-Wheel or Driver Education alike - was offered "Book Lesson," even though a Driver Education student doesn't book lessons at all. That's fixed now: a Behind-the-Wheel student still sees "Book Lesson" exactly as before; a Driver Education student with no class picked yet sees "Go to Classroom," which takes them straight there; and a Driver Education student who was already assigned a class during creation sees a clean confirmation with nothing further to do - they're genuinely finished in one step.

**The one real student created by mistake during this walkthrough was corrected, not deleted or left wrong.** Her incorrect enrollment was formally ended with a note explaining exactly what happened, and the right one was added in its place - the same way any admin would fix a mis-enrolled student, leaving an honest trail behind rather than quietly rewriting history.

See `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §20 for the exact root causes and how each was live-verified before and after.

### Guardians as First-Class Records
Students can be linked to one or more guardian records (parents/legal guardians), replacing flat emergency-contact strings with structured, searchable, many-to-many data. See `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` for the schema. Key principles:
- **Guardian matching and linking logic lives entirely in the backend service layer** — never in a UI component — so the same logic can be reused by a future public signup form without risking duplicate guardian records.
- **Matching never merges automatically.** Searching by name/email/phone surfaces candidate guardians with disambiguating context (which students they're already linked to); a human always makes the explicit decision to link.
- Minors require at least one linked guardian before their program can be marked complete (not before creation — a new minor student can exist guardian-less while the guardian is being set up, surfaced via a `needsGuardian` flag).
- **Multi-guardian support:** a student can have any number of linked guardians (e.g. divorced parents, or a parent plus a grandparent who does pickups), not just one. Exactly one guardian per student can be marked primary; setting a new primary demotes the previous one transactionally — the UI never shows two primaries.
- **A minor's last guardian cannot be unlinked.** Once a minor is down to exactly one linked guardian, the unlink action is disabled in the UI (with an explanation) and rejected by the backend (`unlinkGuardianFromStudent` in `studentGuardianService.ts`) even if called directly — the client-side disable is a UX courtesy, not the enforcement point. A minor's *second-to-last* guardian can still be unlinked freely; only the very last one is protected. An unknown/null date of birth is treated as a minor for this check, matching the existing `needsGuardian` convention.
- **Staged (create mode) vs. immediate (edit mode) guardian actions:** the reusable `GuardianSubPanel` component renders the same linked-guardian list and row actions (add, unlink, change relationship, set primary) in both places, but the two modes wire those actions differently:
  - **Edit mode** — the student already exists, so every action calls the API immediately (`guardiansApi.linkToStudent`/`unlinkFromStudent`/`updateRelationship`/`setPrimary`) and invalidates the guardian queries. This is what lets a student created *before* the guardian feature shipped (every seeded student) finally get a guardian through the UI — the guardian type-ahead picker is no longer gated to create-only.
  - **Create mode** — nothing is persisted until the student form is submitted. Adding a guardian stages it in local React state; unlinking, changing relationship, and setting primary all just mutate that staged list. On submit, every staged guardian is sent in a single `guardians: [...]` array to `POST /students/with-guardian`, created in one atomic transaction alongside the student (Constraint A — see below). The duplicate-guardian confirm step still runs per staged guardian at staging time, and staging the same guardian twice is blocked locally before it ever reaches the server.
- A combined `GET /search/people` endpoint searches students and guardians together by name, email, or phone, so front-desk staff don't have to choose a page before searching.
- **Atomic creation:** `POST /students/with-guardian` creates a student and creates-or-links **one or more** guardians (`guardians: [...]`, 1..N) in a single database transaction — a failure at any step (including a failure partway through the guardian array) rolls back everything, so a student can never end up saved with a guardian half-linked, and no earlier guardian in the array is left orphaned either. Duplicate guardian references within one request (same `guardianId`, or the same new-guardian email/phone) are rejected before the transaction opens. This is additive alongside the original `POST /students`, which is unchanged and still used whenever no guardian is being linked at creation time (adults; minors whose guardian setup is deferred to a walk-in-style follow-up).
- **Full frontend UI**: a Students | Guardians segmented tab (sharing the Students page's shell, not a new nav item), guardian detail with linked-students and an "Enroll another student" action, a student-form guardian type-ahead (walk-in flow) and guardian-first prefilled enrollment (phone-call flow), a duplicate-guardian confirmation step, sibling display, and the unified cross-entity search wired into the shared search bar.
- **Progressive emergency contacts:** the free-text emergency-contact fields (structurally separate from a linked guardian record) are collapsed behind an "Add an emergency contact" checkbox — unchecked by default on a blank form, checked automatically when the student already has emergency-contact data so nothing existing is ever hidden. The backup/secondary contact is only offered once the first contact has a name or phone. A "Same as guardian" shortcut copies from a linked/staged guardian; with exactly one guardian it copies immediately, with two or more it reveals a radio list so the user picks which guardian to copy from. Unchecking the main checkbox never clears already-entered data.

### Booking Workflow Improvements
The ranked slot-search wizard (`SmartBookingForm`) remains the only path to a booking and the 6D engine remains the only availability source — these additions change how a user *arrives* at a search or a confirm, never how availability or conflicts are computed.

- **Date-range control.** The setup step's search window is no longer a hardcoded "tomorrow + 14 days." Three server-computed preset chips — **Next 2 Weeks** (default, matches the old hardcoded behavior exactly), **This Month**, **Next Month** — plus an always-visible From/To date pair that acts as the readout for whichever preset is active. Editing either date directly switches the selection to **Custom** automatically. All three preset boundaries are computed server-side, in the tenant's own timezone, by `backend/src/utils/tenantTime.ts` (`tenantTomorrow`/`addTenantDays` for Next 2 Weeks, `tenantMonthBoundaries` for This Month, the new `tenantNextMonthBoundaries` for Next Month) and served once via `GET /availability/date-presets` — the browser never computes a boundary itself. The chosen range is passed to the existing ranked-slots endpoint as explicit `startDate`/`endDate` (replacing the old opaque `dateRange: number`); an inverted range or a span over 180 days is rejected server-side (180 days chosen to comfortably cover multi-month advance planning like a state permit/road-test window, while still bounding the per-day-per-instructor scan against an unbounded "anytime this year" query).
- **Book another lesson (same student, same session).** After a booking is confirmed, the wizard now shows a success step offering **Book Another Lesson** instead of immediately closing. Choosing it keeps the student, instructor selection, duration, lesson type, time preference, and date range exactly as they were, clears only the booked slot, and returns to **slot selection** (not setup) with the list refreshed — the just-booked slot is gone and any newly created conflict is reflected, because it's the same `handleFindSlots` re-search the wizard already uses for stale-slot recovery. This is repeatable for a third, fourth lesson; each one is still an independent, fully conflict-checked create through the normal booking path — nothing is batched. **Done** stays a one-click exit at any point. The one visible tradeoff: a page that shows a single "booking confirmed" toast (e.g. the Lessons page) still shows just one toast for the whole session, not one per lesson booked via the loop.
- **Book again (returning customer).** A **Book Again** action on a student's record (visible only once that student has at least one prior lesson) opens the booking wizard prefilled from their most recent lesson — instructor, duration, lesson type, time preference (bucketed from the lesson's start time), and pickup address — landing on the **setup** step, ready to search, not auto-searched. The previous instructor is preselected as the continuity default but stays a free, changeable choice via a normal dropdown (including "any available instructor"); this is deliberately distinct from the Reschedule flow's locked instructor display, which is unaffected. A student with no prior lessons opens the wizard with no prefill — never an error state.

### Instructor Availability: Weekly Checkbox Grid
Editing an instructor's recurring weekly schedule was previously a one-day-at-a-time form (`AvailabilityEditor`) that only exposed a start time — the day's `end_time` was silently computed from the tenant's default max-students-per-day, lesson duration, and buffer time, so "I work 8am–2pm on Fridays" simply couldn't be expressed; the school could only ever describe a day's length via student capacity. A second, read-only component (`AvailabilityCalendar`) rendered directly below it as a "Weekly Calendar View," showing the same rows a second time.

Both are replaced by `WeeklyAvailabilityGrid` (`frontend/src/components/scheduling/WeeklyAvailabilityGrid.tsx`), a single component that both edits and displays the week: one row per day of week, a checkbox for "works this day," and — on checked days — real, independently-editable **start time**, **end time**, and **max students** inputs. Unchecking a day collapses it to a muted "Not working" label; a summary line ("4 days · 26 hrs") totals the checked days live. A per-row **copy to all checked days** action bundles that row's start time, end time, and max students onto every other checked day, entirely in local state — no request fires until the admin clicks **Save Week**, which saves the whole week in one call.

- **`end_time` is now real, not inferred.** The 6D engine's slot generator (`schedulingService.ts`'s `findSlotsInBlock`) was already treating a block's `end_time` as an authoritative ceiling — it has never derived it from `max_students` — so this was purely a frontend change: the grid's own end-time input is now the only source of what gets saved, and shortening a day's window immediately shrinks what the ranked slot search offers for it (verified live: a 60-minute lesson fits a shortened 09:00–10:00 window, a 120-minute lesson correctly does not).
- **Unchecking preserves the row.** A day's checkbox toggles `instructor_availability.is_active`, never a delete — re-checking within the same editing session instantly restores that day's last-known times from local component state. (`is_available`, a second boolean on the same table, is legacy/unused by the application layer and untouched by this feature.) The underlying row does still survive, inactive, across a page reload — but since `GET /availability/instructor/:id` (a shared read used elsewhere) only ever returns active rows, a fresh page load can't pre-fill an unchecked day's old times; "restores the previous times" is scoped to the current session, not persisted across a reload.
- **Bulk save, at most one row touched per day.** `PUT /availability/instructor/:instructorId/week` (`availabilityService.setWeekAvailability`) validates all seven days before opening a transaction — one invalid day rejects the whole request, there is no partial-week save — then upserts the single newest active row per day (`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`, then `UPDATE` or `INSERT`). Any additional pre-existing active row for that day (manually-created split-shift data, which the schema has always allowed via the absence of a `(instructor_id, day_of_week)` uniqueness constraint) is left completely untouched rather than being merged or destroyed — this grid deliberately doesn't build split shifts or the seasonal `effective_from`/`effective_until` date ranges the table also supports, and is designed not to corrupt either if they're ever used by a future feature or direct data edit.

### Tenant Settings: Default Lesson Cost & Timezone Auto-Detect
- **Default lesson cost.** `tenant_settings.default_lesson_cost` (a discrete typed numeric column, `NOT NULL DEFAULT 150` — no settings jsonb, matching `default_hours_required`/`standard_lesson_length_minutes`) prefills the booking wizard's Confirm step cost field via `useTenant().settings.defaultLessonCost`, replacing the old hardcoded `50`. The field stays freely editable per lesson; "Book Another" resets it back to the tenant default rather than the old hardcoded fallback. No backfill — existing lessons keep whatever cost was recorded at booking time, since this setting only affects what a *new* booking's cost field starts at. Configurable in Settings → General → Training Defaults, alongside the other two tenant defaults. Postgres numeric columns serialize as strings over the API (e.g. `"150.00"`), so both the wizard and the Settings form explicitly coerce with `Number(...)` before use — reading this value directly without coercion will throw on any code path that calls `.toFixed()` on it.
- **Timezone auto-detect (convenience only).** A newly created tenant's `tenant_settings.timezone` now starts genuinely `NULL` (the column's DB-level default was dropped in migration 011) instead of silently defaulting to `'America/Los_Angeles'` at row-creation time — this makes "never configured" a real, distinguishable state rather than indistinguishable from "explicitly chose Pacific." While `timezone` is `null`, the Settings page's timezone picker shows a suggestion banner using the browser's `Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone`, clearly labeled "Suggested, based on your browser," with an explicit **Use this timezone** button — it never auto-applies, and accepting it is still just an ordinary form edit that only takes effect once the admin clicks Save. `backend/src/utils/tenantTime.ts`'s `resolveTenantTimezone()` remains the sole authority for what an unset timezone resolves to for real date math (falls back to the same `DEFAULT_TENANT_TIMEZONE` constant as before); detection is never read by anything other than that one Settings-page suggestion. Existing tenants already sitting at the old default value are not backfilled to `NULL` — there is no way to tell whether that was ever an explicit choice, so they simply never see the suggestion.

### Lesson Review Queue, Inline Status Control, and No-Show Fee Flags
Before this feature, there was no way to mark a lesson completed — `LessonModal` had no status control, and the only status-changing entry points were three icon buttons on the `Lessons.tsx` rows, none of which recorded who made the change or enforced any transition rules. Every lesson stayed `scheduled` forever, so completed-lesson filters, student progress, and DMV hours tracking could never advance.

- **Status transitions now record who and when.** `lessons.reviewed_by`/`reviewed_at` (nullable, matching the existing audit-column pattern) are set by all three status-transition endpoints — `POST /lessons/:id/complete`, `/no-show`, `/cancel`. A lesson already in a terminal status (`completed`/`cancelled`/`no_show`) cannot be transitioned again through these three endpoints — correcting one requires an explicit new action, rejected with a `409` naming the current status. `updateLesson`'s generic `PUT /lessons/:id` is unaffected — it stays available for correcting other fields regardless of status.
- **Cancellation unified onto one endpoint.** The old `DELETE /lessons/:id` (no audit trail, no fee-window check) is replaced entirely by `POST /lessons/:id/cancel`, used both for cancelling an upcoming lesson and for retroactively correcting a past one from the review queue. It always records the reviewer, and always runs the fee-window check (see below) — which only ever actually produces a flag when the lesson's start is still in the future, so a past-lesson correction naturally produces no flag ("hours until start" is negative).
- **Review Queue** (`GET /dashboard/review-queue`, `frontend/src/pages/ReviewQueue.tsx`) lists lessons still `scheduled` whose end time has already passed — tenant-timezone-aware, resolved via `backend/src/utils/tenantTime.ts`, never client-side — grouped by day, most overdue first. Each row offers one-click Completed/No-show/Cancelled; each day group has "Mark all completed" (a loop over the same `completeLesson` service call, so the transition guard and fee-flag side effects apply identically to bulk and single actions). A day more than 24 hours overdue gets warning styling. A Dashboard alert ("N lessons need review") opens this view, mirroring the No-Show alert's anatomy. The query accepts an optional `instructorId` from day one, so scoping to a single instructor's own lessons later is a controller-only change.
- **Inline status control on the Lessons page.** Clicking a scheduled lesson's status badge (table or card view) opens a small menu — Completed/No-show/Cancelled — calling the same mutations the existing row/card icon buttons already use. The Lessons table's Actions column is now sticky (`position: sticky; right: 0`), so it no longer scrolls off-screen on a narrow viewport.
- **Lesson completion mode setting.** `tenant_settings.lesson_completion_mode` (`'manual'` | `'auto'`, default `'manual'`) is stored and selectable in Settings, but only `'manual'` (the review queue above) is implemented — no job reads `'auto'` yet. The Settings UI notes this explicitly so it isn't mistaken for live automation.
- **No-show / late-cancellation fee flags — a flag, never a payment.** For this school, the instructor collects a no-show or late-cancellation fee in cash, and it never reaches the business. A fee is therefore recorded as a row in a new `fee_flags` table (amount, reason, source lesson, resolution status) — never a payment record, never revenue, and deliberately never referenced by `instructorService.getInstructorEarnings` or any student revenue column. Marking a lesson `no_show` sets an outstanding flag for the amount configured in `tenant_settings.cancellation_fee_amount`; cancelling a lesson within `tenant_settings.cancellation_fee_window_hours` of its start does the same; cancelling outside the window sets nothing. Completing a student's next lesson clears *every* outstanding flag for that student at once ("cash assumed settled" is one event, not per-incident). A flag can be waived with attribution (who, why), and — only when `tenant_settings.cancellation_fee_payee` is `'school'` (re-checked server-side, not just hidden client-side) — converted into a real payment record via the existing payment-creation path, at which point the flag is marked `paid` and kept, not deleted. With the default `'instructor'` payee, that conversion endpoint is unreachable (`403`) and no payment record is ever created. Surfaced prominently but non-blockingly: a banner on the student record (with waive), a banner in the booking wizard (pure display — no button is ever disabled because of an outstanding fee), and a read-only, never-summed list on the instructor record.

### Instructor Licensing and Service Areas
`instructorService.createInstructor`/`updateInstructor` previously never read `instructor_license_number`/`instructor_license_expiration` at all — both columns already existed on the `instructors` table, but the form's "License Information" section collected a value the backend silently discarded on every save, the same silent-drop bug class previously fixed for `employmentType`. Both fields are now wired through create, update, and read.

The tracked credential is specifically the **Driving School Instructor License** (California DMV) — the license to *teach* behind-the-wheel lessons, renewed every 3 years via exam or an 18-hour DMV-approved continuing-education program — not a **Driving School Operator License** (which licenses whoever manages the school itself; a school needs both, and they can be different people). The form section is labeled with this exact term rather than the previous ambiguous "License Information," with a caption clarifying it isn't a driver's license. `instructors.drivers_license_number`/`drivers_license_expiration` also exist on the row and remain intentionally unwired in the UI for now — a distinct credential, not a more-specific version of the instructor license, left for a future feature to pick up without a schema change.

**Service areas, and why they replace an earlier abandoned attempt.** Instructors previously had only a single `zip_code` (their address) and no concept of a service area, so the ranked slot search surfaced every active instructor regardless of where they actually work. A disconnected prior attempt at this already existed in the frontend — `Instructor.homeZipCode`/`serviceZipCodes`, a free-text comma-separated field, fully wired in the form's local state but with no backing database column, so it was silently discarded on every save exactly like the license fields above. `CHANGELOG.md`'s `[0.5.7]` entry documents that attempt as shipped, referencing a migration that was never actually created in this repo, and describing a **hard filter** ("instructors who don't serve the area are filtered out") that would have made an instructor disappear from search the moment the feature was configured for anyone. That dead UI was removed rather than built upon — but the first real implementation shipped that exact same hard-filter failure mode anyway, in a subtler form (see below); it's fixed now.

The real implementation is `instructor_service_areas` (see ARCHITECTURE.md §10), a normalized table of `(instructor_id, zip_code)` rows rather than a comma-separated column or a `jsonb` array — membership needs to be a plain indexed lookup usable directly inside the ranked-slots query, which a comma-separated column can't provide without a fragile prefix-match scan, and a `jsonb` array gets no referential integrity or index-backed equality test for free. Managed on the instructor record via a simple add/remove zip-chip UI (`frontend/src/components/instructors/InstructorServiceAreas.tsx`), saved as a full-list bulk replace.

**Service areas rank, they never exclude.** An earlier version of this feature filtered results to in-service-area instructors and fell back to everyone only if that filtered set was completely empty. That design was broken by construction: an instructor with no configured service area always counts as in-area (Constraint B below), so a single unconfigured instructor kept the filtered set non-empty forever — the fallback branch essentially never fired, and any instructor who *did* configure a service area could be silently excluded from every search, including when they were the closest available match. Configuring a service area could therefore only ever hurt an instructor. Confirmed live: the nearest instructor to a student disappeared from results entirely while unconfigured instructors ranked above him. Fixed: service-area membership is now a pure ranking/display signal, never a filter.
- An instructor with no configured service-area zips serves every zip — the backward-compatible default. Existing instructors do not disappear from search the moment this feature ships.
- The ranked slot search *always returns every candidate instructor's slots.* In-service-area slots sort above out-of-area slots; within each group, the existing proximity-then-date ordering applies unchanged.
- Area membership decides sort position only, never inclusion — a slot is never dropped because of service-area configuration.
- Proximity scoring already measures "how close" an instructor's starting point is to the pickup address; a hard filter on top of it added exclusion risk (as above) without adding any ranking value a comparator couldn't already express.

### Instructor License Expiry Notifications
A lapsed **Driving School Instructor License** (California DMV — see the entry above for why this specific credential, distinct from a Driving School Operator License, and its 3-year renewal cadence) is a compliance risk a school can be fined for. Before this feature, `instructors.instructor_license_expiration` persisted correctly but nothing read it proactively — the only way to notice an approaching or lapsed license was opening that instructor's record individually.

**Not built on `notificationProcessor`.** The task that specified this feature named "the existing notificationProcessor cron" as the mechanism to extend, but direct inspection showed that doesn't exist as a usable target: `notificationProcessor.ts` is an email-send worker over a `notification_queue` table where every row requires a `lessonId` (a lesson-reminder pipeline, not a general queue); that table has no migration anywhere in this repo and does not exist in the live database; and `startNotificationCron()` is never called anywhere in the app, so no cron of any kind was actually running. This feature adds a **new** cron (`backend/src/jobs/instructorLicenseCron.ts`, same `node-cron` shape, genuinely wired into `index.ts`), writing to the real, already-working `notifications` table — the same one the existing no-show alert already uses successfully — rather than the broken `notification_queue`/email pipeline.

**Escalating thresholds, then a post-expiry cadence.** Admins (owner + admin role users) are notified at 180/90/30/14/7 days before expiry, then every 7 days after expiry, indefinitely, until the license is renewed to a future date.

**Fires exactly once per threshold, per instructor, per expiration date.** A new table, `instructor_license_notifications` (see ARCHITECTURE.md §10), records every threshold that has already fired, keyed `UNIQUE (instructor_id, expiration_date, threshold)`. This unique constraint — not an application-level check — is the actual dedup enforcement: the cron always attempts an `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, and only creates the admin-facing notifications when that insert genuinely records a new row. Because `expiration_date` is part of the key, **updating an instructor's expiration to a new date resets the schedule for free** — the new date has no matching rows yet, so every threshold fires fresh against it, while rows for the superseded date are left in place (audit trail, matching this codebase's "never delete, only supersede" pattern elsewhere — `fee_flags`, `student_guardians`) but are permanently irrelevant, since nothing ever queries by a stale date again.

**Tenant time, not process time (Constraint A).** "Days until expiry" resolves through a new `tenantTime.ts` primitive, `daysBetweenTenantDates`, composed entirely from the module's own existing exports — never `new Date()` or local getters. `instructor_license_expiration` is a plain `date` column with no time component, so this is pure calendar-day string arithmetic once the cron has already resolved "today" in the tenant's own timezone.

**Dashboard alert is live-computed, not persisted-state-driven.** `GET /dashboard/license-expiry-alerts` recomputes "which active instructors have an expiration within 180 days, or already past" fresh on every call — the same pattern the review queue uses, not the no-show alert's join-against-dismissal-state pattern. There is no "dismiss" concept: the alert simply stops appearing once an expiration is edited to a comfortable future date. Danger styling applies once expired or within 30 days (matching the existing Permits Expiring tile's own cutoff); warning otherwise.

**Always visible on the record and list, not just the alert.** The instructor record and the Instructors list both surface the same status derived client-side from the already-tenant-resolved `tenantNow.today` (`frontend/src/utils/licenseExpiry.ts`, the same safe category as `turning18.ts`'s predicate — comparing already-resolved strings, never deriving "now" itself). An instructor with **no expiration recorded** is flagged `missing`, styled with the same danger weight as `expired` — never silently treated as compliant just because there's no date to compare against.

### Fixing Three Real Bugs From a Codebase Health Audit

A full health audit surfaced three confirmed, live bugs. None of them are related to each other, and each shipped as its own commit.

**Every lesson booking's confirmation and reminder emails had silently never been queued, in this app's entire history.** The database table they get written to had simply never been created - not a partial fix, not a typo, just genuinely never built, even though the code that writes to it has existed all along. Because that write was wrapped in a "don't let this break the actual booking" safety net, nobody ever saw an error - the booking always succeeded, the reminder just quietly never got queued behind the scenes. The notification history page had the identical problem from the other direction: it always failed to load, for the same missing-table reason. Both are fixed now, and along the way two more small things were fixed to make that possible - a page that reads notification history was never actually sending its own login credentials, so it would have failed to load even with the table fixed, and one of the database queries referenced a lesson field by the wrong name. Automatic sending itself is still not wired up - that was true before this fix and remains true after; it's flagged as a separate piece of follow-up work, not silently bundled in here.

**Two edits that looked like they worked, but didn't save anything.** Changing a student's date of birth on their record, and changing a lesson's pickup address after it was already booked, both appeared to save successfully - no error, no complaint - but neither one actually changed anything in the database. Both are now genuinely fixed.

**A tenant's own basic info was quietly never loading correctly - the exact same bug this app already fixed once before, in a different file.** Every time the app started up, it asked the server for the tenant's business name and a few other basic details, and the server handed back the right data in the wrong shape - so the app never actually saw it, and silently fell back to a generic placeholder name instead. Nobody would have noticed, because the fallback looked reasonable enough on its own. This is now fixed, and the fix was checked carefully both ways: confirmed nothing on the app's side had already grown its own workaround for the bug, and confirmed the fix didn't accidentally miss a related field that turns out to have never existed in the database at all (that one's now flagged separately, since adding it would be a new decision, not a fix to something broken).

See `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §22 for the exact root causes, the schema, and how each was live-verified before and after.

### Two Safety Fixes to the Blockchain Groundwork That Isn't Turned On Yet

This app has real, working groundwork for a future blockchain feature - but that feature has never actually been switched on, and won't be for this launch. A second pass of the same health audit checked how that dormant groundwork was built, since "not turned on" should genuinely mean nothing about it can affect the app today, and found two things worth tightening up before going further.

**One file was quietly allowed to reach around the safety wall instead of going through the front door.** This app is built so that every single piece of blockchain-related code has to pass through one specific, deliberately narrow gateway - the idea being that as long as nothing bypasses that gateway, flipping the feature on or off is genuinely just one switch, nothing more. One file had found its own way around that gateway instead of using it, sitting right next to a line of code doing it the correct way. It's fixed now, structurally - nothing about what actually happens when booking a lesson has changed, today or ever, since the feature stays off either way. Along the way, a second, unrelated, already-existing problem was found and is being reported rather than silently patched: the specific bookkeeping table this code writes to doesn't actually exist yet in the database, so that particular write has quietly never done anything, before or after this fix - worth a decision later, not part of what this fix set out to do.

**A private key must never simply be typed into a log file, and this app used to do exactly that.** If this blockchain feature is ever turned on without a wallet already set up for it, the app used to just generate a brand-new one on the spot and print it straight into its own output - including the secret part, which is the one part that should never appear anywhere by accident. Most hosting providers keep that output around indefinitely, so a real secret typed into it that way could sit there forever, discoverable long after anyone remembers it exists. That's removed entirely. In its place: if the feature is ever turned on without a real key already configured, the app now simply refuses to start at all, with a plain, direct explanation of exactly what's missing and what to do about it. Nothing is ever generated quietly on the app's behalf anymore - a real key has to be a deliberate choice made by an actual person, every time. None of this changes anything about how the app runs today, since this whole scenario only exists once someone deliberately turns the feature on.

See `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §23 for the exact mechanics and how each was live-verified.

### Spring Cleaning: Dead Code, Unused Dependencies, One Bad Test, and a Few Stale Sentences

A third pass of the same health audit turned into general housekeeping - clearing out code and dependencies that had genuinely stopped being used, fixing one test that had quietly stopped meaning anything, and correcting a handful of places where the written word no longer matched the running app.

**A fair number of functions, one whole file's worth of unused constants, and a small pile of dependencies turned out to be dead weight - all removed, only after checking each one fresh.** Nothing here was taken on faith from an earlier pass; every single item was re-searched for real usage right before it was touched, precisely because two earlier rounds of work had already changed some of the same files - and one item that looked dead before genuinely wasn't anymore (a function a previous fix had since written a real test around), so it was left alone exactly as found. Six backend packages and one frontend package that nothing actually imports are gone too, along with the code that never used them.

**One test was quietly incapable of ever failing, and it's fixed now.** It checked a number against a rule computed from that same number - which will always agree with itself, no matter what the underlying logic actually does. Worse, the specific example date it used had drifted into the past since it was written, so the real-world scenario it claimed to test didn't even exist anymore - and the test still passed regardless, because it was never actually checking anything real. It's rewritten now to check against an honest, fixed expected answer, the same way the test right next to it in the same file always correctly has.

**A few places where the documentation had quietly drifted from the truth were corrected.** The technical architecture document used to open by describing a whole second database and an entire separate indexing service as if they were already running - they aren't, and the app's real, complete database today is just the one it's always had. That's been rewritten to say so plainly, and to frame that content honestly as a possible future direction rather than something built. A changelog entry still described an old design for matching instructors to their service area that was replaced before it ever really shipped - it now describes what the app actually does. And two small code comments that had quietly gone stale - one describing a folder of test scripts as far narrower than it's actually grown into, another implying a save button does something it's never actually done - were corrected to say what's really true today, without changing anything about how either one behaves.

See `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §24 for the full list and the evidence behind each decision.

## 1. The 6-Dimensional (6D) Scheduling Engine

BDP implements a novel scheduling algorithm designed for high-availability fleets.

### Technical Specification
The engine resolves availability by checking six independent data dimensions:
- **I**nstructor (Schedule + Time Off)
- **V**ehicle (Fleet Availability)
- **S**tudent (Personal Schedule)
- **W**orking Hours (School Policy)
- **B**uffer Time (Transition Logistics)
- **C**apacity (Max students per day)

### Implementation Note
BDP is **Capacity-Based** within an explicit time window: admins set both a start time and an end time for each working day (see § Instructor Availability: Weekly Checkbox Grid), and within that real window the engine automatically generates up to the configured capacity's worth of lesson slots, never letting a generated slot run past the window's own end time.

## 2. Merkle Tree Micropayment Aggregation

To enable sustainable micropayments (1-10 satoshis) without losing revenue to miner fees, BDP utilizes **Merkle Batching**.

### The Innovation
1.  **Leaf Generation:** Each protocol action (booking, payment, certify) generates a deterministic SHA-256 hash.
2.  **Aggregation:** Hashes are accrued in the database until a batch threshold (1 hour or 100 actions) is met.
3.  **Merkle Commitment:** A binary Merkle tree is built. The **Root Hash** is committed to the BSV blockchain in a single OP_RETURN transaction.
4.  **Verification:** Schools and students can verify their individual action against the on-chain Merkle Root using a provided proof path (sibling hashes).

### Economic Impact
- **Without Batching:** ~4,500 satoshi loss per 100 actions due to miner fees.
- **With Merkle Batching:** ~98% profit margin for the protocol.

## 4. Identity & Verifiable Credentials (BRC-52)
BDP will issue digital certificates for:
- Course Completion (Students)
- Teaching Credentials (Instructors)
- Fleet Maintenance Records (Vehicles)

These credentials are cryptographically signed and stored in a privacy-preserving format, allowing for instant third-party verification (e.g., by insurance companies or DMVs) without sharing PII.

**Course completion certificates attach to an enrollment, not a person** — a student who completes both driver education and driver training earns two certificates on two different DMV form types, which a person-level certificate couldn't express. This part is now built: see "Certificate Issuance Tracking" under Built Features above and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §13 for the operational tracking feature (recording, void accounting, withdrawal transcripts), including a `completion_hash` computed and stored at record time, matching enrollment completion's own forward-compatibility pattern (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §12). **The BRC-52 cryptographically-signed, third-party-verifiable credential described in this section remains future work** — no blockchain code exists for certificates yet, `ledger_txid` stays permanently `NULL`, and today's tracking is a plain operational compliance record, not a signed verifiable credential. See `docs/BLOCKCHAIN.md`.
